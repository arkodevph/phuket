Property & Ownership Notice

Practical points for Australians considering a Thailand property purchase. Obtain independent advice before committing.

Last reviewed: 11 October 2026

Scope and details to confirm

This is general information, not an assessment of your eligibility or of any individual property. Ownership law and transfer requirements must be checked with an independent Thai lawyer and the relevant Land Office at the time of purchase.

Area guides and illustrative images

The website currently presents Phuket neighbourhood guides and illustrative images. These are not verified property listings, photographs of an available property, offers for sale or representations about a property’s title or foreign ownership eligibility. No verified property inventory or sale prices have been published.

If a property is later offered, confirm the seller’s authority, exact unit, availability, title, tenure, foreign quota, approved plans, condition, charges and contract independently. A villa, apartment or condominium label alone does not establish what you can legally own.

Foreign freehold condominiums and the quota

Eligible foreign buyers may own a registered condominium unit on a freehold basis under Thai law. Foreign ownership is generally limited to 49% of the total floor area of all units in the condominium. The quota is measured by area, not simply by the number of units, and it is not a promise that a particular unit is available for foreign ownership.

Your eligibility, available quota at transfer, condominium registration and required evidence of the source or remittance of funds must be verified. Ask your independent Thai lawyer and the condominium juristic person to confirm the documentation needed by the Land Office before signing or transferring money.

Official background: Thai government guidance on transfer documents and the foreign ownership quota. Confirm the law and local requirements that apply to your purchase; a general guide cannot replace that check.

Land, villas and leasehold

Foreigners are generally restricted from owning land in Thailand. Limited statutory exceptions can have substantial conditions and permissions; this website does not establish that you qualify for an exception. A description of a villa or estate does not mean a foreign buyer can own the underlying land.

Leasehold gives contractual rights that differ from freehold ownership. Do not assume a renewal, resale right or long-term arrangement is guaranteed. Obtain independent advice about registration, enforceability, duration and the rights over both the building and land. Do not assume a nominee or company arrangement is a lawful route around foreign ownership restrictions.

Official background: Thai government overview of foreign property ownership and restricted land acquisition.

Prices, currency, finance and returns

Where a property price is quoted in THB with an approximate AUD equivalent, treat the AUD amount as an indication only. Confirm the quoted exchange rate and date, the actual purchase currency, bank fees, taxes, transfer costs, maintenance or common-area charges and any other costs. Exchange rates can change before payment.

Investment outcomes, rental income, occupancy and capital growth are not guaranteed. Rental and property management rules depend on the property and applicable law. A cash or finance preference in the enquiry form does not assess affordability, arrange a loan or confirm that finance is available to a foreign purchaser.

Independent advice before a commitment

Before paying a reservation fee or deposit, engage an independent Thai property lawyer to check title, encumbrances, seller authority, contractual protections, ownership eligibility and transfer procedures. Seek qualified Australian and Thai tax, financial or credit advice where relevant to your circumstances.

Buying property does not itself guarantee a visa, residency or permission to work in Thailand. Verify immigration rules separately. Confirm the business’s legal identity, role, licensing where required, any fees or commissions and any referral arrangements before engaging a paid service.
